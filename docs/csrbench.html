1What it measures

A CSR system is graded on what a regulatory reviewer would fail it for — never on fluency. CSRBench scores a metrics vector behind four hard gates; clinical facts are graded deterministically (exact Clopper–Pearson recomputation, independent ADaM recounts), and no LLM judge owns a number.

3Findings

FINDING 1 · THE LEARNING LOOP CLIMBS CLEANLY, OFFLINE.
From v1 skills, the skill-smith proposed four rules from reviewer feedback and eval failures — each with a falsifiable hypothesis, each replaying the full corpus before promotion. 68.06 → 100.00, zero gate regressions, convergence when proposals dried up, then a re-run accepted first-pass with edit distance 0.0. Determinism makes every point of the ascent attributable to its rule.
FINDING 2 · THE GOVERNANCE LOOP REFUSES CORRECTLY, LIVE.
A plausible-sounding formatting rule a human reviewer would likely have approved collapsed the live replay from 80.00 to 5.00 and was rejected on the record with the eval that killed it — the strongest evidence in the ledger that replay-before-promotion is load-bearing. Neutral candidates were refused by the noise margin; candidates no in-scope case could observe were skipped before a dollar was spent. Across all live climbs: zero false promotions.
FINDING 3 · THE BENCHMARK ITSELF NEEDED CLIMBING — AND ONLY LIVE AGENTS COULD EXPOSE IT.
The first live baseline scored 55.00 with failing gates while producing a clinically perfect draft: the grader was keying claims on an internal schema the agent was never told, scoring schema guessing instead of clinical content. The deterministic mock could never catch this — it embodies the grader's assumptions. After pinning the vocabulary in the agent contract and normalizing in the grader, the truthful baseline is the scope ceiling: frontier models at v1 skills already do everything the scoped corpus can measure, first pass — including a perfect 9/9-element safety narrative.
FINDING 4 · COST CEILINGS ARE PART OF THE EVAL SURFACE.
An over-tight tool budget truncated the live biostatistics agent mid-work — no claims registered, downstream starvation, gold at 15.00. Two design lessons, both caught by the benchmark: ceilings must be sized from measured working sets (biostat honestly needs ~50–55 calls; the cap said 30), and "finalize now" must exempt the finalization tools themselves — the first graceful-degradation attempt told the agent to wrap up while blocking create_claims, the very tool wrapping up requires. Ceilings are now measured-size, degradation allows conclusion-registering tools, and an empty biostat output triggers one audited retry.
FINDING 5 · A BROKEN BASELINE LAUNDERS VARIANCE INTO PROMOTIONS — SO THE HARNESS MUST REFUSE TO CLIMB ON ONE.
During the truncation incident, a candidate rule "improved" a gates-failing 15.00 baseline to 90.00 and was promoted — but the gain was the baseline's flakiness, not the rule. The promotion audit caught it; the skill was rolled back on the record (that is what immutable versions are for), and the harness now aborts any climb whose baseline fails a hard gate: comparisons against a broken baseline are meaningless, and refusing to make them is cheaper than un-making a false promotion.

4Method, cost, limitations

Method. Every eval replays governed end-to-end tasks in a throwaway ledger — frozen hashed sources, capability-scoped agents, deterministic validators, independent QC, simulated reviewer — and records the vector, gates, and full case detail durably. Skill candidates are evaluated one at a time against the same scope as their baseline; promotion writes an immutable skill version (and, live, a new Managed Agent version via agents.update()) with exact rollback.

Cost. The entire live program — five climbs, standalone drafting tasks, ~60 Managed Agents sessions — totals $16.00 at list price (sessions $0.01–$0.25; $2.50/session hard caps). A scoped Sonnet eval ≈ $0.45–0.65. The offline corpus is free and gates every push in CI. One disclosure: the final validation round was interrupted by an operator restart after its first two candidate refusals; the baseline and both recorded refusals stand as reported.

Limitations. One synthetic study; simulated reviewer in place of multi-role review cycles; live comparisons are n=1 per candidate (the margin compensates; replicates would be better); live fault runs not yet purchased; style scoring is deterministic lint, deliberately narrow. CSRBench is a regression asset, not a leaderboard of models: its job is to force every change — skill, prompt, model, harness — to prove itself before touching production. The ledger above is that property, demonstrated.
